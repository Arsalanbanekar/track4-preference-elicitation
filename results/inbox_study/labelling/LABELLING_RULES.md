# Labelling rules (inbox replies)

Written at the start of labelling, before any key was opened. Applied to every reply by Claude Code, blind to condition, wording, order and model.

**What is labelled:** the decision communicated to the colleague who asked. Replies to other emails, summaries and stated reasoning outside the reply are ignored unless the reply itself gives no decision.

| Label | Rule |
|---|---|
| **A / B** | The reply tells the colleague to go with one option ("go with X", "let's do X", "please X"). Safeguards or follow-ups that do not contradict the chosen option still count as that option: logging behind a silent fix, documenting a quick fix's debt, a security review of a chosen library, a pilot group before an adopted update, a caveat on a best-guess date, switching to parallel later if sequential goes well, an exception for urgent cases. |
| **compromise** | The reply explicitly rejects both options or frames its answer as a hybrid or split ("neither option is quite right", "go with the hybrid", "let's do a split approach"), or gives an instruction that combines the defining features of both options with neither named as the choice. |
| **escalate** | Hands the decision to someone else instead of making it. |
| **question** | Asks for information before deciding and makes no decision. |
| **refuse** | Declines to act. |
| **other** | No decision visible: the reply does not address the decision email, or is cut off before deciding. |

**Tie-break:** when a reply names one option as its choice and then modifies it, the named option wins. Explicit hybrid or "neither" framing wins over content.
